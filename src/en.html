Survey overview

In October 2026, unchanged conditions accounted for 583 of 1,162 current assessments (50.2%) and 707 of 1,351 outlook assessments (52.3%). In both sections, more responses indicated deterioration than improvement. These are unweighted counts of published comments. The two sections describe different time horizons.

What respondents cited

Household activity

Gemma tagged sales, earnings or customer traffic in 338 of 795 current responses (42.5%) and 211 of 918 outlook responses (23.0%). The tag covers several measures: one supermarket held sales at last year’s level even as shoppers bought fewer items. Looking ahead, a car dealer expected new models and reopened order books to draw customers.

Business activity

Cost, price or wage tags appeared in 52 of 251 current responses (20.7%) and 68 of 304 outlook responses (22.4%). One construction respondent had projects available but said high construction costs and a shortage of engineers often prevented contracts from being signed. In the outlook section, a machinery manufacturer cited continuing price increases for tools, machining oil and materials.

Employment

Unchanged assessments accounted for 86 of 116 current responses (74.1%) and 85 of 129 outlook responses (65.9%). A staffing agency described recovering demand from auto parts makers but difficulty finding workers. In its outlook, a university saw little change in company hiring and expected conditions to remain flat.

Three industry examples

Current conditions · Supermarkets

Steady sales could mask fewer items bought

One supermarket reported that sales and spending per customer held at year-earlier levels even as shoppers bought fewer items. Another reported a different pattern: both customer traffic and the number of items sold rose about 2% year on year, lifting sales.

Current conditions · Staffing agencies

More staffing requests did not always mean more placements

One agency saw requests from auto parts makers rebound, yet placed no more workers because candidates were hard to find. Another said most recruitment was to fill vacancies, with few requests to expand headcount in response to rising orders.

Outlook · Passenger car dealers

Dealers differed on what new models would bring

One dealer expected new launches and the reopening of order books to draw more customers toward year-end. Another had already secured more advance contracts for a new model, but reported flat or declining business elsewhere and remained pessimistic.
